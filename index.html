AI coding for problem solving

Example tools

I made these as examples: small tools chosen because the problem behind each one is common to most nurseries. Each was built by describing the problem, in plain English, to an AI coding assistant. They are free, run on a phone, and need no accounts. Some we use every day at our farm; others were built for this session to show the pattern. Take them as starting points: open the one closest to your own problem, then change it to fit the way you work, the same way the tools we use on our farm were shaped to fit us.

🌡

Degree Days

Field · pest management

“Which pests should we be scouting for this week?”

Adds up growing degree-days from the weather at your own location and shows which pests are active now, which are coming, and what plants to check. Pest timings from the Rutgers Nursery & Landscape Pest Scouting Guide.

Open the tool → Read the prompts

Built in a day: the brief, then each request in order.

📋

Scout Report

Field · scouting

“Scouting notes end up on paper, then retyped into a spreadsheet that only one person can open at a time, usually from a desk. Can the report go straight from the phone into a sheet everyone can see?”

A scout picks the farm, location and crop, taps each pest’s severity and distribution, adds a photo — and every pest becomes a row in a shared Google Sheet the whole team can see at once. Works with no signal.

Open the tool → Read the prompts

Includes the first real test and what it changed.

SprayCast

Field · spraying

“When can we actually spray this week?”

Judges every hour of the forecast at your farm against your own limits for wind, gusts, rain chance and temperature, shows which way drift would go, and puts planned sprays on your calendar.

Open the tool → See the history

It started as one HTML file uploaded by hand — the first commits.

🌿

BlightCast

Field · disease risk

“Is the weather building boxwood blight risk, and are we still protected from the last spray?”

Runs the published boxwood blight risk model on the weather at your town, against your own alert level, with a spray log that shows how long you are covered.

Open the tool → See the history

Also began as a single HTML file, then grew.

🥶

Temp Alert

Field · frost and heat · in progress

“We don’t have a weather station or a monitoring system. Can we still get an email when it is about to freeze?”

Watches a nearby NEWA or other public weather station every 15 minutes and emails the right people when it reads too cold or too hot — and when the station stops reporting. Runs in the nursery’s own Google Sheet, with no sensors to buy.

Open the tool → Read the prompts

Shows the “a web page can’t wake you up” moment — what needs to run on a schedule.

🌸

PlantPicks

Marketing · events

“People at our new-plant talk want to rate plants and keep notes, and we want to know what they liked.”

A follow-along for a presentation: every plant numbered as in the slides, star ratings that come back to the nursery, private notes that stay on the phone, and a copy you can send yourself.

Open the tool → See the history

Built for one open house, then kept live.

How to read “how it was built”

  1. Prompts (Degree Days, Scout Report, Temp Alert): the brief, then each request in order — what was asked, in the words it was asked.
  2. History (the others): every saved step, oldest at the bottom. Each one is a change someone asked for and then checked.
  3. None of them was right first time. Most of the history is trying it, finding what was wrong, and asking again.
